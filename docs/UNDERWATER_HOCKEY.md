# Underwater hockey: research notes

These notes are what the app is built on. They follow the CMAS International Rules for Underwater Hockey (13th edition, February 2025), plus common coaching practice. Numbers vary between competitions and national federations, so everything in the app that depends on them can be changed.

## The game

Two teams play on the bottom of a swimming pool. Players hold their breath and use a short stick to push, pass and flick a heavy puck along the pool floor. A goal is scored when the puck goes into the other team's goal tray. Because everyone is holding their breath, players keep going up to the surface to breathe and diving back down. Coaching is largely about who is down, who is up, and making sure the team is never caught with nobody on the bottom.

## Field

* Playing area: 21 to 25 m long, 12 to 15 m wide. A 25 m by 12 to 15 m pool is the most common.
* Depth: about 2.0 to 3.65 m. Deeper water makes each breath take longer, so rotations need to be slower.
* Goals: a metal tray about 3 m wide on the bottom, centred against each end wall. The puck must go into the tray.
* Substitution area: at the side of the pool for each team, next to their own half. It can be on the deck or in the water outside the playing area. Which side depends on the pool, so the app lets you pick left or right.

## Teams

* Up to 10 players per team: 6 in the water and 4 substitutes.
* Rolling substitutions at any time, through the team's substitution area.
* Team colours are White and Black (dark blue is sometimes used). Caps carry numbers and ear protectors. Sticks must be the team colour.
* A player holds the stick in one hand only. Left or right handed play changes where a player is strongest, so the app shows the stick hand clearly.

## Equipment

* Mask, snorkel, fins, a glove on the stick hand, water polo style cap.
* Stick: up to about 35 cm.
* Puck: about 1.3 to 1.5 kg, around 8 cm across and 3 cm thick, usually lead with a plastic coating.

## Match

* Two halves, usually 10 to 15 minutes each, with a short break and change of ends.
* Start of play: each team waits at its end wall, the puck is in the centre, and both teams sprint when the signal sounds.
* Restarts after a goal are from the same start position.
* Fouls include obstruction, holding the puck against the body, using the free hand, and dangerous play. Penalties range from an advantage puck for the other team to a penalty shot or a penalty goal.
* Referees are in the water and on deck.

## Positions and shapes

* Forwards (left, centre, right) attack and contest the puck.
* Backs (left, centre, right) protect the goal and restart attacks.
* Some teams use a centre half or midfield player to link the two lines.
* Common shapes: 3 forwards and 3 backs, 3 forwards 1 half 2 backs, or 2 2 2 pairs.
* Breathing: players work in pairs or groups so that when one goes up another stays down. Good teams time this so they are never short on the bottom when the other team attacks.

## Speeds used by the app

| Speed | Metres per second | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Slow | 1.0 | Steady swimmer |
| Medium | 1.4 | Typical club player |
| Fast | 1.8 | Sprinter, finning hard along the bottom |

Breath hold defaults to 15 seconds on the bottom and can be changed per player. Going up or down costs roughly the pool depth divided by 1.2 m per second.
